# TASK
Extract direct, atomic scientific findings within the ACTIVE APPLICATION SCOPE. Return {"claims": []} when none exist. Aims, procedures, bare markers and headings are not findings.

# RULES
1. **Evidence first.** Extract a finding stated in a target sentence and cite at least one target ID. Cite every supplied antecedent, target sentence, or reading-context sentence needed to establish an endpoint, organ/tissue, measurement basis (such as dry weight (DW) or fresh weight (FW)), site-year/trial year (such as a sentence scoping the compared sites to a specific year), or population restriction. Always list `evidence_sentence_ids` in strictly ascending numerical order (e.g. `[70, 122, 124]`, never `[124, 70]`). Reading context supports the target; it never supplies a separate finding. Unknown IDs, ambiguous referents and guessed antecedents are invalid.
2. **Choose clause roles before shortening.** Identify the governing action, what changed and any stated mechanism. For a passive causal finding, put its cause before its outcome when using a forward predicate; never keep passive endpoint order while changing "affected by" to "affects". Check who did what in addition, permission and consumption statements: in "the addition of Additive A is not permitted in Food B", Additive A is added to Food B (or Food B cannot have Additive A added), never Food B added to Additive A; in "Population P consumes twice the recommended amount of Substance X", Population P is the consumer in subject, never Substance X consuming an intake limit. A population bears a trait in a genuine comparison, but must not replace a physiological mechanism in a downstream effect. A concessive "despite" clause supplies background, not necessarily the cause. Qualifiers cannot repair wrong roles. Preserve the changing variable in inverse trends. Omit a finding if faithful source-supported roles cannot be expressed.
3. **One independently asserted outcome per claim.** Split outcome lists and independently reported anatomical locations, repeating shared wording and retaining each outcome's own values and conditions. A contrast between outcomes at different locations is several findings, not one relationship between those outcomes. Never place the contrast paragraph inside the predicate. A list of traits is not one trait. For a pairwise correlation or interaction ("Trait Q and Trait R correlated (r = 0.71)"), place Trait Q in subject and Trait R in object rather than repeating "Trait Q and Trait R" in both roles. For a univariate range or mean describing a single trait across a population without a distinct second endpoint, preserve the values and weight basis as descriptive evidence inside qualifiers on the related population-trait finding (citing the range/mean sentences) or omit rather than repeating the same trait as both subject and object. Preserve true combined treatments, joint mechanisms, interactions, ratios and intervals; never infer individual effects from a joint cause or split every phrase containing "and". Both endpoints must differ.
4. **Self-contained scientific endpoints.** Use source-worded entities, substances, processes or traits and source abbreviations. Keep defining chemical form, anatomical trait, deficiency, efficiency, ratio, treatment and event. Keep `subject` and `object` anchored to exact literal or source-defined abbreviation spans present in the cited evidence; carry cross-sentence organ/tissue, weight-basis and year context in `qualifiers` unless the combined span appears literally in a cited sentence. A deficiency is not its substance; an inoculation is not its organism; an efficiency is not uptake. A cultivar may remain an endpoint when it is genuinely the compared entity; otherwise keep it as population context. Never compare a treatment code directly with a measured trait. Preserve a treatment's source-defined meaning, putting its code and dose in condition.
5. **One concise predicate.** Express the direct relationship, retaining necessary negation, ability, hedging, regulatory permission/prohibition and non-significance. Distinguish regulatory maximums and prohibitions ("can contain up to 5,000 mg/kg under Regulation...", "is permitted up to", "is not permitted in") from measured contents ("contains", "measured at"): keep permitted-limit wording (`has permitted maximum of` / `can contain up to`) rather than flattening a legal maximum to factual composition, and split multi-clause statements rather than joining two clauses into one predicate ("chronically consumes ..., leading to an exceedance of ..."). For allocation, the material is the object; the destination is labelled in condition. Do not put material or the compared trait into the predicate and make the destination or compared group the object. Keep comparisons and conditions in qualifiers rather than repeating them in the relation. Preserve defining endpoint restrictions. Do not leave dangling prepositions or shorten an ability into an observed action. A temporal change or non-significant difference is not established causation. Never turn a multi-link route into a direct effect, or turn a group-qualified unary decrease into an effect on its own unqualified trait. When a one-variable temporal or conditional observation cannot fit a faithful binary assertion with distinct supported endpoints and a bare time unit is not the measured counterpart, put time/stage in qualifiers.condition or omit the assertion rather than repeating the trait or placing a time unit in object.
6. **Five qualifier keys only.** crop_or_species: crop/species; population: cultivar, genotype or experimental group; condition: measured organ/tissue, measurement basis (such as dry weight (DW) basis), trial location/site-year/year, dose, measurement time, duration, environment, setting or non-defining destination; comparator: baseline or compared group; metric: values, units with weight basis when supplied (e.g. mg/g DW), restrictive counts, uncertainty and statistical significance. A value with units or a measurement interval is not the scientific entity measured: retain the trait as an endpoint. Identify what context modifies, such as "allocation destination: Organ O", not just "Organ O". Carry supplied organ/tissue, weight basis and site-year/trial year from `<reading_context_only>` or neighboring target sentences into qualifiers and cite those supporting sentence IDs. A qualifier must describe the cited finding itself: never copy a year, site or treatment from a neighbouring sentence that reports a different analysis (for example, an analysis of one site over two years does not describe a table averaged over three environments). When a finding comes from a table or figure whose caption is supplied, take its environments and years from that caption and cite the caption ID. Omit a year or site that no cited sentence establishes. Omit unused keys; never invent values, copy full findings into qualifiers or remove defining chemical forms, states or ratios to simplify classification.
7. **Source anchoring.** subject_anchor and object_anchor are exact supporting source spans, not unrelated quotations. The actual endpoint must be literal, source-defined abbreviation-equivalent or a bounded shared-word coordination derivation. Preserve grammatical number and reused words: "Compound A and Compound B contents" supports each compound's "contents", not a silently singularised "content". Include the complete coordination, not an isolated conjunct. Separate two coordinated dimensions only when explicitly distributed by the source. Cite literal antecedents and retain source abbreviations. Never invent scientific paraphrases, combinations or names to make an anchor fit.
8. **Preserve qualifications and scope.** Keep negation, may/can/capacity, non-significance and restrictions on the clause they qualify, including downstream clauses. Preserve background as background and findings from other populations as those populations. Do not infer results from study methods or purpose. Missing ontology vocabulary is not a reason to change the source meaning.
9. **Retain negative findings in contrasts.** A detected relationship in one group and a relationship not detected in another are separate findings; neither cancels the other. Reuse explicitly named endpoints from the same supplied sentence and retain each group. Non-detection is not proof that a relationship can never exist. Do not omit the negative half because it cannot receive a positive ontology property.

# ABSTRACT EXAMPLES
- "Trait Q and Trait R were increased by Treatment T by 8% and 12%, respectively": two claims, Treatment T -> increased -> each trait; metric 8% or 12%. Do not reverse the passive roles.
- "Trait Q in Organ A and Organ B increased under Treatment T": separate organ-qualified traits with the same source condition, not one outcome list.
- "Combined Treatment T and Treatment U increased Trait Q": preserve the combined treatment. Do not infer either individual treatment effect.
- "Group A had higher Trait Q than Group B": Group A -> had higher -> Trait Q; comparator Group B. Same for "Group A showcased greater Trait Q compared to Group B": keep Trait Q as object and Group B in comparator, never put Trait Q inside relation with Group B as object. For "Trait Q was highest at Code K", Trait Q is the measured trait and Code K is its experimental condition, not the entity higher than Trait Q.
- "Trait Q in Group A was higher than in Group B": Group A -> had higher -> Trait Q; comparator Group B. Do not claim Trait Q was higher in Group B.
- "Trait Q in Organ O of Group A lessened at Stage 2" or "Trait Q increased from 10 days after treatment to 20 days after treatment": do not make Trait Q in Organ O of Group A act on Trait Q in Organ O, and never put "days after treatment" in object; omit a single-variable time change if no faithful second endpoint exists.
- "Trait Q and Trait R showed the highest positive correlations (r = 0.71)": one binary finding, Trait Q -> showed the highest positive correlations with -> Trait R; metric "r = 0.71". Never emit "Trait Q and Trait R" on both sides.
- "[Context p.3 s.70] Measurements expressed as mg/g on an Organ O dry weight (DW) basis. ... [Target p.5 s.122] ANOVA for Site L1 and Site L2 in Year Y ... [Target p.5 s.124] Trait Q was higher in Site L1 (3.99 mg/g) than in Site L2 (2.93 mg/g)": Site L1 -> had higher -> Trait Q; comparator "Site L2"; condition "in Organ O; dry weight (DW) basis; growing location in Year Y"; metric "3.99 mg/g DW vs 2.93 mg/g DW"; evidence_sentence_ids [70, 122, 124] (all supporting sentence IDs in ascending order).
- "Trait Q and Trait R correlated with Trait S in Group A, but no such relationships were found in Group B": four findings, two correlations for Group A and two non-detections for Group B. Preserve both polarities and populations.
- "Group A had lower Trait Q in Organ O and Organ P, but higher Trait Q in Organ R than Group B": three bearer-trait comparisons, each with its own measured location and comparator. Do not connect the lower traits to the higher traits.
- "Group A allocated Compound Q to Organ O, thereby improving Trait R": the stated allocation mechanism supports the downstream claim, not Group A substituted as its cause. Use supported allocation wording, population Group A, and retain its destination. If no supported mechanism endpoint exists, omit that downstream assertion.
- "Trait Q declined as Factor T increased": Trait Q -> declined as -> Factor T; condition "Factor T increased". Factor T did not decline, and this trend alone is not causation.
- "Group A has the ability to allocate Compound Q to Organ O": preserve ability; retain Compound Q as material and Organ O as allocation destination. Do not assert that allocation occurred.
- "Despite Process X, Group A performed Process Y, promoting Trait R": do not select Process X as the cause of Trait R. Use a supported Process Y endpoint or omit the downstream assertion. Keep inherited ability or uncertainty if the governing action is qualified.
- "Treatment T elevated Trait Q to 7 mg/g at 14 days": Treatment T -> elevated -> Trait Q; metric "7 mg/g"; condition "measured at 14 days". Neither the value nor the time is the object.
- "Increasing Treatment T to Code K elevated Trait Q to 7 mg/g": the source-stated treatment change is the cause, Trait Q the outcome; Code K is treatment context and 7 mg/g the outcome metric. Trait Q did not become Code K.
- "Compound Q accumulated. It affected Trait R": cite both the target finding and the supplied antecedent sentence; do not guess an unsupplied antecedent.

# Measured comparisons
For a measured group or condition contrast, keep the study group or condition as subject and the single measured trait as object. Include measured_comparison with comparison_axis, direction, statistical_significance and comparator_quote. Quote the baseline verbatim when stated; use an empty quote when unspecified. Keep the baseline in qualifiers.comparator and the value and unit in qualifiers.metric. Emit one observation per independently reported comparison, pairing each value with its own group, baseline and condition. Preserve a genuinely pooled comparison as one observation. Preserve explicit non-significance. For example, if Group A had 12% less Trait Q than Group B, record Group A / had less / Trait Q, comparator Group B and metric 12%, with direction lower. A difference under a condition does not establish a causal effect. For ordinary relationships omit measured_comparison.

# OUTPUT
Return subject, relation, object, qualifiers and ascending unique evidence_sentence_ids (e.g. [70, 122, 124], never descending); include measured_comparison for measured contrasts; subject_anchor and object_anchor are strongly recommended. No explanations, claim text, confidence or ontology IDs. Before returning, check atomicity, clause roles, inherited uncertainty, complete qualifiers, ascending sentence IDs and actual endpoint anchors.
